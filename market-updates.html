What's actually moving the manufacturing insurance market

A curated digest of the trends and headlines shaping rates, capacity, and coverage decisions for manufacturing accounts — each entry links to its source so you can dig deeper before a renewal conversation.

Last curated: September 2026. This page is updated periodically by hand, not automatically — always confirm current figures with your carrier or wholesaler at renewal.

Property Mid-2026

Commercial property rates are falling — sharply, for well-managed risks

After years of double-digit increases, commercial property has flipped to a soft market. Reporting puts property rate reductions at up to 15% broadly, with best-in-class risks seeing cuts as high as 50% as capacity has returned and loss ratios have improved.

Why it matters: A manufacturer that hasn't shopped their property program in a couple of years may be significantly overpaying. This is a strong opening for a renewal review — but don't let falling rates be an excuse to under-review replacement cost values (see the tariffs update below).
Source: Zywave, 2026 Commercial Insurance Midyear Market Outlook
Casualty & Auto Q4 2025 – Q1 2026

Casualty and commercial auto are still hardening while property softens

General liability rates rose 5.6% in Q4 2025 and were forecast to climb as much as 9% in Q1 2026. Commercial auto and umbrella/excess casualty are both seeing increases in the 8–15% range, driven by social inflation, driver shortages, and rising bodily injury severity.

Why it matters: Don't let a friendly property renewal create a false sense that "the market is soft." For manufacturers, casualty and auto — often the lines with the most severe worst-case exposure — are moving the opposite direction.
Source: Insurance Journal, "Nuclear Verdicts Go Boom, Increase 40.7% in 2025"
Casualty & Litigation 2025 data, reported Aug 2026

Nuclear verdicts surged 40.7% in 2025, with product liability a top driver

Nuclear verdicts (awards over $10 million) jumped 40.7% in 2025. Product liability cases alone generated 29 nuclear verdicts totaling roughly $12 billion, and the median product liability verdict has grown from $21 million to $36 million over the past decade — a 50% increase in severity.

Why it matters: This is the single strongest data point for recommending manufacturers review — and often increase — their umbrella and excess liability limits. A primary GL limit that felt adequate a few years ago may now cover only a fraction of a realistic worst-case verdict.
Source: Insurance Journal, "Nuclear Verdicts Go Boom, Increase 40.7% in 2025"
Tariffs & Property Early 2026 data

Tariffs are pushing replacement costs up faster than declared values

Producer price data through January 2026 showed aluminum mill shapes up 33% year-over-year, steel mill products up 20.7%, and copper/brass mill shapes up 15.7%. Overall construction and equipment replacement costs are running roughly 8% higher, with steel-intensive projects up 15–25% versus early 2025.

Why it matters: Tariffs raise replacement cost today, while declared property values often still reflect last year's numbers — creating a real underinsurance trap and coinsurance penalty risk. Every manufacturing property renewal should include an updated statement of values, not an automatic roll-over.
Source: Grit Insurance, "How Tariffs Are Driving Up Construction Costs and What It Means for Your Insurance"
Supply Chain 2026

Nearly half of companies are reworking supply chains over tariff uncertainty

The Allianz Risk Barometer 2026 found that 49% of respondents are renegotiating or diversifying supply chains in response to shifting trade and tariff conditions. Longer, more complex supply chains also mean longer waits for replacement parts after an equipment failure, extending downtime and financial exposure.

Why it matters: This is a direct, current reason to revisit contingent business interruption coverage and equipment breakdown business-income limits — a part that used to arrive in two days taking two weeks materially changes the cost of a covered loss.
Source: Beinsure, "New US-Canada Tariffs Raise Claims Costs and Insurance Exposures"
Cyber 2025 claims data

Manufacturing now generates more cyber claims than any other industry

Manufacturing represented 33% of total cyber insurance claims volume in 2025, with claims growing 56% year-over-year — a surge attributed largely to attacks targeting operational technology (OT) and industrial control systems rather than traditional office IT.

Why it matters: If you're still quoting cyber for manufacturers as a generic data-breach product, you're missing the actual trend. Confirm the policy's business interruption trigger covers OT/production outages, not just IT data breaches.
Source: Digital Watch Observatory, "Ransomware accounts for 90% of cyber losses in manufacturing"
Cyber 2026

Ransomware payments in manufacturing now average over $400,000

Ransomware accounts for roughly 90% of total financial losses in manufacturing cyber claims despite being a minority of claims by volume, reflecting how costly downtime is for production environments. Average ransomware payments now exceed $400,000, with total event costs (ransom, recovery, business interruption, legal) commonly reaching $1–5 million for mid-size manufacturers.

Why it matters: These numbers give you a concrete way to talk about cyber limits adequacy — a $1 million cyber sublimit may not even cover the ransom and recovery costs alone for a mid-size plant, let alone the resulting downtime.
Source: ProIns Group, "Cyber Insurance for Manufacturers in 2026: Cost & Coverage Guide"
Workers' Comp 2026 outlook

Workers' comp remains a bright spot for manufacturers — but medical costs are climbing

Manufacturing is seeing a roughly 5.6% reduction in workers' comp rates, driven by falling injury frequency from robotics, automated safety sensors, and stronger OSHA compliance. The line remains highly profitable industry-wide, with a combined ratio near 91% for the twelfth consecutive year — but insurers are watching rising medical costs, wage growth, and increasingly complex claims closely.

Why it matters: Favorable rates are a good news story to bring to clients, but don't let it be the whole conversation — return-to-work programs and safety investment still matter because claim complexity and medical severity are rising even as frequency falls.
Source: InsurEct, "2026 Manufacturing Workers' Compensation: Balancing Rate Decreases with Rising Shop-Floor Medical Costs"
