Commercial Auto & Inland Marine

8 min read · 5-question knowledge check

Not every manufacturing exposure sits inside the four walls of the plant. Delivery vehicles, field service trucks, and goods or tools that travel between job sites all need coverage that a property or GL policy doesn't provide. That's where commercial auto and inland marine come in.

Commercial auto: who's exposed

Any manufacturer that owns delivery trucks, provides field service, or has sales reps driving company vehicles needs a Commercial Auto policy covering:

  • Liability — bodily injury and property damage the manufacturer's vehicles cause to others.
  • Physical damage — collision and comprehensive coverage for the manufacturer's own vehicles.
  • Hired and non-owned auto — covers liability when employees use rental cars or their own personal vehicles for company business, a gap easy to miss when a company doesn't think of itself as having a "fleet."

Why this matters for you as a producer

Commercial auto is one of the few lines still hardening even as much of the broader market softens, driven by a shortage of skilled drivers, rising bodily injury claim costs, and nuclear verdicts in auto liability suits. For a manufacturer with even a modest delivery fleet, this means auto renewal conversations deserve real attention — not an assumption that last year's premium will roughly repeat.

Inland marine: coverage that moves with the property

Inland marine fills a gap that neither property nor auto coverage is designed for: property that moves, or property that belongs to someone else while it's in the manufacturer's care. Common manufacturing applications include:

  • Transit coverage — goods in transit between the plant, warehouses, and customers, whether on the manufacturer's own trucks or a common carrier.
  • Contractors' equipment — portable tools, generators, and equipment used by field service or installation crews away from the main location.
  • Bailee's customers coverage — when the manufacturer holds a customer's property for processing, repair, or storage (common in contract manufacturing and toll processing), this covers that property while it's in their custody.
  • Installation floater — covers equipment or materials from the time they leave the manufacturer's control through installation at a customer site.

Why this gets missed

Inland marine is easy to underinsure because it doesn't map neatly to one obvious policy the way property or auto does — it's often assembled from several floaters and forms tailored to the specific ways a manufacturer's goods and equipment move. A thorough risk assessment call should always ask: What leaves this building, who else's property comes into this building, and what happens to both while they're in transit?

Key terms

Hired and Non-Owned Auto
Coverage extending liability protection to vehicles the business doesn't own but uses for business purposes, like rentals or employee personal vehicles.
Inland Marine
A category of coverage for property in transit, property of others in the insured's care, and mobile equipment — distinct from ocean marine.
Bailee's Customers Coverage
Coverage for a customer's property while it is in the insured's custody for processing, repair, or storage.
Installation Floater
Coverage for materials or equipment from the point they leave the manufacturer through completion of installation at a customer site.
