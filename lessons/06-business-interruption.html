Business Interruption & Supply Chain

9 min read · 5-question knowledge check

Property insurance rebuilds the building. Business interruption (BI) insurance — more formally, business income coverage — keeps the company alive while that rebuilding happens. For a manufacturer running on thin margins and long customer lead times, the income coverage can matter more than the property claim it's attached to.

What business income coverage pays for

  • Lost net income the business would have earned had the loss not occurred, based on prior financial performance and probable future performance.
  • Continuing normal operating expenses, including payroll, that keep running whether or not the plant is producing anything.
  • Extra expense — costs incurred specifically to keep operating or to speed recovery, like renting temporary space or expediting equipment repair.

Coverage runs for the period of restoration — the time it should reasonably take to repair or replace damaged property and resume operations — plus, if purchased, an extended period of indemnity that continues coverage for a set number of days after operations resume, recognizing that customers and revenue don't snap back instantly.

Contingent Business Interruption (CBI)

Here's the exposure many manufacturers miss: what happens if their plant is fine, but a key supplier's plant burns down, or a major customer's facility floods and stops ordering? Standard BI only responds to damage at the insured's own locations. Contingent Business Interruption extends coverage to lost income caused by physical damage at the premises of a specified supplier, customer, or other dependent business.

Why this matters for you as a producer

Roughly half of manufacturers report actively renegotiating or diversifying supply chains in response to tariffs and shifting trade conditions — a clear signal that supply chain fragility is top of mind. That makes CBI one of the more underused, and most persuasive, coverage conversations you can have: ask "what happens to your revenue if your top supplier goes down for two months?" and most manufacturing owners don't have a confident answer without this coverage.

Civil authority and ingress/egress coverage

Two narrower but valuable extensions round out the BI conversation:

  • Civil authority coverage — responds when a government order prohibits access to the insured's premises because of damage to nearby property (e.g., a chemical spill down the street shuts down the block).
  • Ingress/egress coverage — responds when physical damage prevents access to or from the insured's location, even without a formal government order.

Getting the numbers right

Business income limits should be based on a worksheet reflecting actual projected revenue and expenses, not a rough guess. Undervaluing this coverage is common because it's harder to visualize than a building limit — but an inadequate business income limit can be just as damaging as an inadequate property limit, especially for a manufacturer with a long period of restoration due to specialized, hard-to-replace equipment.

Key terms

Period of Restoration
The time reasonably required to repair or replace damaged property and resume normal operations, during which business income coverage applies.
Contingent Business Interruption (CBI)
Coverage for lost income caused by physical damage at a specified supplier's, customer's, or other dependent business's premises.
Extended Period of Indemnity
An optional extension continuing business income coverage for a set number of days after operations resume, covering the ramp-back period.
Civil Authority Coverage
Coverage responding when a government order restricts access to the insured's premises due to nearby property damage.
