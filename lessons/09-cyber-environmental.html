Cyber Liability & Environmental Risk

9 min read · 5-question knowledge check

Two exposures often get treated as afterthoughts on a manufacturing account, and both deserve more attention than they usually get: cyber risk to production systems, and environmental liability from the materials manufacturers actually handle.

Why manufacturing is now a leading cyber target

Manufacturing has become one of the most attacked sectors for ransomware, and the reason is specific to how plants operate: production lines have almost no tolerance for downtime, which makes manufacturers more likely to pay a ransom quickly to restore operations. Ransomware now accounts for the large majority of total financial losses in manufacturing cyber claims, even though it's a minority of claims by count — when it hits, it hits hard, frequently driving average claim severity over $1 million once ransom, recovery, business interruption, and legal costs are combined.

IT risk vs. OT risk

Manufacturers face cyber exposure on two fronts:

  • IT (information technology) — standard office systems, email, customer and financial data. This is the exposure most cyber policies were originally built around.
  • OT (operational technology) — the industrial control systems, PLCs, and SCADA systems that actually run the production line. An attack here doesn't just steal data — it can physically halt manufacturing, and increasingly, it's the primary target.

Why this matters for you as a producer

Manufacturing claims volume in cyber has grown sharply year over year, driven specifically by attacks on operational technology. When quoting cyber for a manufacturer, ask whether the policy's business interruption trigger extends to OT/production system outages, not just IT data breaches — a policy that only responds to a data breach may leave the manufacturer's biggest actual exposure, a halted production line, uncovered.

What a manufacturing cyber policy should include

  • Ransomware/extortion payment coverage
  • Business interruption from a network security failure, ideally extending to OT/production systems
  • Data breach response costs (notification, credit monitoring, forensics, legal)
  • Contingent business interruption if a key vendor's systems (e.g., a cloud ERP provider) are compromised

Environmental and pollution liability

Standard CGL policies broadly exclude pollution-related claims. For manufacturers that use solvents, coatings, chemicals, or generate industrial waste, this exclusion leaves a real gap that needs to be addressed with a dedicated pollution liability policy, covering:

  • Cleanup costs for contamination on or migrating from the insured's site.
  • Third-party bodily injury and property damage from pollution conditions, including gradual releases that a standard GL "sudden and accidental" pollution exception wouldn't cover.
  • Business interruption resulting from a pollution event requiring the facility to shut down.
  • Emerging contaminant exposure, including PFAS ("forever chemicals"), which is drawing increasing regulatory and litigation attention across multiple manufacturing sectors.

Key terms

Operational Technology (OT)
The industrial control systems, PLCs, and SCADA systems that run physical production processes, as distinct from office IT systems.
Ransomware
Malicious software that encrypts or locks a victim's systems until a ransom is paid, often combined with data theft and extortion threats.
Pollution Liability
Coverage for cleanup costs and third-party claims arising from pollution conditions, generally excluded from standard CGL policies.
PFAS
Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, a class of persistent industrial chemicals facing growing regulatory scrutiny and litigation.
