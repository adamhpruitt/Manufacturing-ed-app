Building a Complete Insurance Program

10 min read · 5-question knowledge check

You've now covered every major coverage line a manufacturer needs. The last skill to build is pulling them together into one coherent program — and knowing what to ask on a risk assessment call so nothing falls through the cracks.

The nine lines, in one view

  1. Commercial Property — building, BPP, and stock, valued at replacement cost with accurate reporting.
  2. General & Product Liability — premises/operations plus products-completed operations, written on an occurrence form.
  3. Workers' Compensation — correctly classified payroll, with attention to the experience mod and return-to-work programs.
  4. Equipment Breakdown — closing the mechanical/electrical failure gap left by property forms.
  5. Business Interruption & CBI — income protection for both the insured's own losses and dependent supplier/customer losses.
  6. Commercial Auto — owned fleet plus hired/non-owned exposure.
  7. Inland Marine — goods in transit, contractors' equipment, and property of others in the manufacturer's care.
  8. Umbrella & Excess — the layer that actually protects against a catastrophic verdict.
  9. Cyber & Environmental — OT-aware cyber coverage and pollution liability for the materials actually handled.

The risk assessment questions that tie it together

A strong first meeting with a manufacturing prospect or renewal should surface answers to all of these:

  • What exactly do you make, and what raw materials, chemicals, or processes are involved?
  • What would happen to revenue if your single largest piece of equipment went down for a month?
  • Who are your key suppliers and customers, and what happens if one of them can't operate?
  • Do your products ship to other states or countries, and what's your product liability history?
  • Do you have delivery vehicles, field service techs, or equipment that leaves the building?
  • Do you hold any of your customers' property for processing, repair, or storage?
  • How is your production line controlled — and is that control system connected to the internet?
  • Have you updated declared property values for current material costs and tariffs?

Why this matters for you as a producer

The manufacturers who get underinsured aren't usually the ones who ignored insurance — they're the ones whose program was built one line at a time over many years, with nobody stepping back to check whether the pieces still fit together. A producer who runs this full checklist at every renewal, not just at the point of sale, becomes the trusted advisor instead of just the incumbent quote.

Staying current

Rates, capacity, and litigation trends move fast, and manufacturing sits at the intersection of several markets moving in different directions at once — property softening, casualty and auto hardening, cyber claims rising specifically in manufacturing OT systems, and tariffs reshaping replacement costs. Check the Market Updates page regularly so your renewal conversations reflect what's actually happening in the market today, not what was true a year ago.
