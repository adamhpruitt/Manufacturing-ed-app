Equipment Breakdown Coverage

7 min read · 5-question knowledge check

Ask a plant manager what keeps them up at night, and "the main press seizing up" ranks a lot higher than "a fire." Yet standard property policies — even Special Form — exclude the exact failures manufacturers worry about most: mechanical breakdown, electrical arcing, and motor burnout. That gap is filled by Equipment Breakdown coverage (still sometimes called Boiler & Machinery, its historical name).

What's excluded from property, and why EB exists

Standard property forms are built around external perils — fire, wind, theft, water. They exclude loss caused by the equipment's own internal failure: a motor burning out from an electrical short, a boiler rupturing from over-pressurization, a control system frying itself. Equipment Breakdown coverage was created specifically to close this gap, and for a manufacturer, it's rarely optional in practice — it's core coverage.

What Equipment Breakdown typically covers

  • Direct damage to the failed equipment itself.
  • Business income and extra expense resulting from the breakdown — often the larger cost, since a failed press or chiller can halt production for days or weeks.
  • Spoilage of perishable stock or work-in-process dependent on temperature or process control (common in food, pharma, and chemical manufacturing).
  • Expediting expenses — the extra cost of rush shipping a replacement part or paying overtime to get a line back up faster.
  • Service interruption — loss resulting from an outside utility's equipment breakdown that cuts power, water, or gas to the insured.

Why this matters for you as a producer

Supply chain delays make equipment breakdown more expensive to recover from than ever. If a replacement part that once shipped in two days now takes two weeks because of tariff-related sourcing disruptions, the business income loss from a single equipment failure grows dramatically. This is a strong, concrete talking point for why EB limits — and the business income period tied to it — deserve a fresh look at every renewal, not just an automatic renewal quote.

How it's typically packaged

Equipment breakdown is usually added to a property policy as an endorsement or written as a companion monoline policy alongside the CPP. Either way, coverage typically applies on a blanket basis to covered equipment at a location, rather than requiring a schedule of every individual machine — though very high-value or specialized equipment may still be scheduled for higher sublimits.

Jointly loss control matters here

Because EB losses often stem from deferred maintenance, most EB carriers include (or offer) engineering-based loss control: infrared thermography scans, vibration analysis, and boiler/pressure vessel inspections. These aren't just paperwork — they catch failures before they happen, and a producer who helps schedule them is protecting the account's loss history, not just checking a box.

Key terms

Equipment Breakdown (Boiler & Machinery)
Coverage for mechanical/electrical failure of covered equipment, excluded from standard property forms.
Service Interruption
Coverage for loss resulting from breakdown of an outside utility's equipment that disrupts power, water, or gas to the insured.
Expediting Expense
Extra cost incurred to speed up repair or replacement after a covered breakdown, such as rush freight or overtime labor.
